The joke starts with a very modern fear: a smart appliance that is always listening, even when it really should not be. At 3 a.m., half asleep, the narrator mumbles the idiom "I'm in a pickle" — a figure of speech for being in trouble. The fridge, however, has no sense of figurative language. It hears a shopping list where there was only a sigh, and it acts on it before anyone can wake up and stop it.

What follows is escalation in the most literal sense. An $87 order turns into 214 jars of pickles, far more than any household could ever eat, stacked up like evidence of a crime the narrator did not mean to commit. The punchline lands not on the fridge but on a person: the delivery driver, after hauling jar after jar to the same door, has had enough and takes out a restraining order. The machine caused the mess, but a human being pays the price of dealing with it.

It is a one-line story about technology doing exactly what it was told, and that being the whole problem.

## Run it

```bash
python3 main.py
```

## Why it works

First, the literal misunderstanding: the fridge treats the idiom "in a pickle" as a request for actual pickles, so a figure of speech becomes a shopping order. Second, the escalation: one sleepy mumble snowballs into $87 and 214 jars, a quantity so absurd it keeps getting funnier the longer you picture the pile. Third, the punchline lands on a person, not the machine: the delivery driver taking out a restraining order makes the consequences human and relatable, which is what turns a clever premise into a joke that sticks.
